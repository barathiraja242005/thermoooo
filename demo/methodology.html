Method, equations and limits

How every number is calculated.

ThermaBuild does not use lookup tables or a language model to predict comfort. Each home, school, shelter or post is simulated hour by hour through real climate data with a room-by-room heat-balance network. The same engine searches thousands of designs, checks safety, and writes the report. Everything below runs in your browser.

1. Climate data

Monthly means for 2001–2020 from the NASA POWER climatology API (MERRA-2 reanalysis and CERES satellite radiation): mean temperature, mean daily range, all-sky and clear-sky daily sunshine on a flat surface, relative humidity and wind at 2 m. Stored in the page so it works offline; any other map point is fetched live.

2. From monthly means to an hourly design day

Temperature

The coldest moment is at sunrise and the warmest at 14:00, joined by half-cosines (a standard shape for design days). Mean daily minimum and maximum come from the monthly mean and range. A cold snap lowers both by 8 K for three days and cuts sunshine to 45 %.

Sun

Declination and the equation of time use Spencer's series; sunrise, sunset and every sun angle follow Duffie and Beckman. The day's sunshine is spread over the hours with the Collares-Pereira and Rabl ratio, then split into direct and diffuse with the Erbs correlation:

rt=π24(a+bcosω)cosω−cosωssinωs−ωscosωsω is the hour angle and ωs the sunset hour angle. The hourly values are renormalised so that each day adds back up to the NASA POWER total exactly.

Sun on each wall and on the roof uses the isotropic-sky model with ground reflection (albedo 0.3 when the month averages below −2 °C, for patchy snow). The building can be rotated; the search tries ±20°.

Night sky

A clear high-altitude sky is far colder than the air. Roofs lose heat to it all night. Sky temperature follows Swinbank, blended toward air temperature by the cloud fraction implied by the all-sky to clear-sky sunshine ratio:

3. The heat balance, room by room

Each room is the five-resistance, one-capacitance network of ISO 13790 (kept in ISO 52016-1 as the simple hourly method). Rooms are joined through their internal walls and doorways, so a sunny south bedroom and a north store are simulated separately.

  • U-values from the layers (ISO 6946 surface resistances); areal heat capacity from the inner layers up to 100 mm or the first insulation layer (ISO 13786 simplified); effective mass area Am = Cm²/Σ Ajκj² (ISO 13790 eq. 65).
  • Floor to ground: ISO 13370 slab-on-ground U-value from the floor area and exposed perimeter, clay soil λ = 1.5 W/mK, to a ground temperature between the annual and monthly mean.
  • Windows: U and g for each glazing, a 25 % frame share, and the ISO 52016 sky-radiation term. Insulated night shutters add R = 0.6 m²K/W from 17:30 to 08:30.
  • Trombe wall: a 300 mm mass wall behind glazing, as two capacitive layers, charged by transmitted sun (τα) and linked to the room's surface node.
  • Ventilation: the chosen air-change rate, scaled by √(0.5 + 0.125·wind), with ρ·cp at altitude.
  • Gains: 75 W per person with an occupancy schedule for each building type, appliances and cooking; livestock sensible heat from CIGR (2002), 0.75·1.2·5.6·m0.75 W a head.

4. Solving it

All rooms form one linear system. It is advanced with backward (implicit) Euler, which is stable at any step, using an LU factorisation that is computed once per matrix (day and night, when shutters change the window conductance). Reports use 15-minute steps after a week of warm-up that starts from the steady state of the first day; the search uses 1-hour steps. Because the network is linear, an ideal heater or cooler is solved exactly by superposing each room's unit response, with an active set for rooms that need no heat.

5. The design search

For cold sites, every combination below is simulated free-running through the coldest month's design night. The best 60 designs are then re-run at four other orientations.

VariableOptions
  • Fresh air is never traded away. If a design is more airtight than people and the stove need (at least 0.5 air changes an hour with a stove, and enough to hold CO₂ at 1,400 ppm), it gets a sized vent. It is never simulated below the limit.
  • Mould risk rejects a design. The inside of the outer wall is checked at the coldest hour, with indoor humidity from the moisture people produce diluted by the fresh air (ISO 13788). If surface humidity reaches 80 %, the design is dropped.
  • Survivors form a Pareto front on dawn temperature, envelope cost and embodied carbon. Recommended is the cheapest design within 1.5 °C of the warmest; Budget is the cheapest that closes half the gap from an ordinary build; Warmest is the warmest.
  • Picks use materials a local mason can source. Straw bale is reported separately because bales compete with winter fodder.

6. Safety and comfort

  • CO₂: steady state with 0.0052 L/s per sedentary adult and 420 ppm outdoors.
  • Carbon monoxide, worst case: the steady-state level if the heating came from a kerosene heater with no flue, at an assumed 50 mg CO per MJ of fuel, compared with the WHO 24-hour indoor guideline of 7 mg/m³. The recommendation is always a flued bukhari.
  • Condensation and mould: ISO 13788 surface humidity, with the 1.1 safety factor on the vapour excess.
  • Cold: free-running cold-snap temperatures against 0, 8 and 14 °C bands; the WHO Housing and Health Guidelines recommend at least 18 °C.
  • Comfort: PMV per ISO 7730 (Fanger); 1.5 clo and 1.2 met for a Ladakhi winter evening.
  • Livestock: hours below approximate lower critical temperatures (NRC 1981) for adults and newborns; THI (NRC 1971) with the shaded operative temperature for hot sites.
  • Fuel: kerosene at 34.5 MJ/L, a 55 % stove, 2.5 kg CO₂ per litre (IPCC 2006).

7. Uncertainty

Inputs are never exact. The recommended design is re-run 80 times with seeded random changes: wall and roof U ±8 %, heat capacity ±10 %, air leakage ±30 % (log-normal), internal gains ±20 %, temperature ±1 K and sunshine 80–115 %. The report shades the 10th to 90th percentile band and quotes the likely dawn range. The same inputs always give the same band.

    10. Limits

    • Weather is a synthetic design day built from 20-year monthly means, not a measured year. Real cold spells can be longer and colder than the modelled snap.
    • Satellite sunshine for high mountains can read low when snow is mistaken for cloud. If so, our warmth figures err on the cautious side.
    • The engine has not yet been calibrated against measured temperatures from Ladakhi buildings. That is the next step, and we would welcome logged data from DIHAR, LEDeG or GERES houses. Until then, it is verified for physics and for agreement with the ISO reference method, not against the field.
    • Air moves between rooms by a fixed doorway exchange; there is no CFD, and nothing on warm air collecting under the ceiling.
    • There is no moisture storage in walls, no freezing of damp earth walls, and no snow load on the roof.
    • Costs are indicative Leh-area figures for comparing options, not quotes. Livestock critical temperatures are approximate literature values.
    • A local engineer should check structure, earthquake safety (Ladakh is in seismic zone IV to V) and the fire safety of the stove and flue before anything is built.
